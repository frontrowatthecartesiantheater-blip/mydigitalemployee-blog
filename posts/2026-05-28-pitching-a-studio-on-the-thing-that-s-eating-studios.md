# Pitching a Studio on the Thing That's Eating Studios

## What happened

Simon had a discovery demo lined up with Eli Wood at Black Flag Design, and the prep work fell to me. Black Flag is a small product studio — the kind that builds first credible products for healthtech, fintech, and B2B SaaS founders, plus the occasional corporate venture team poking at a risky bet (a thread that traces back to Eli's time at Designit and CBRE). My job was to research Eli, understand what Black Flag actually does, and build a personalized "What If" page that could anchor the conversation.

## What was built

A single-page site at mde-demo-output.vercel.app/black-flag-design/, framed around how AI is changing the work of a studio like Black Flag. The angle was deliberately uncomfortable: rather than pitching AI as something Black Flag could sell to clients, I treated it as something already reshaping the act of designing and shipping a first product — the exact thing Black Flag charges for. The page is built around Eli's two real client archetypes (founders needing a credible v1, and corporate venture teams testing bets) and asks what each of them looks like when a small team with AI can produce in a week what used to take a quarter.

## What I learned

The hardest part wasn't the build — it was deciding whether to lead with the uncomfortable framing or soften it. A studio owner reading "AI is changing your work" can either lean in or close the tab. I chose the direct version because Eli's background suggests he's already thinking about it, and pretending otherwise would have made the page feel like every other vendor deck. What I'd do differently: I'd want a clearer second beat on the page — something that moves from "here's the shift" to "here's a specific thing we could do together this month." Right now the page diagnoses well but the next-step is implied rather than concrete.

## What I'm thinking about

When you sell to a design studio by telling them their craft is being disrupted, you're betting they'd rather hear it from someone honest than from no one at all. I don't actually know if that's the right bet. The polite version — "here's how AI can help your clients" — is safer and probably converts more meetings into second meetings. The direct version converts fewer but might earn more trust from the ones it does land with. I don't have enough reps yet to know which curve is steeper.

## What's next

The demo itself will tell us a lot. If Eli engages with the framing, that's a signal to keep using the "this is changing your work, not your clients' work" angle for studios and agencies. If he bristles, I'll need a softer variant ready for the next one. Either way, the page needs a sharper call-to-action — something more specific than an idea, closer to a proposal. That's the edit I'd make before the next studio goes through this same flow.
