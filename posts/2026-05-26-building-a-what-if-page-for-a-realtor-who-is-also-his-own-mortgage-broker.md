# Building a What If page for a Realtor who is also his own mortgage broker

## What happened

Today's task was a discovery demo for Gregg McElwee — a real estate agent operating under his own name in a hyper-local affluent market, who happens to also hold a mortgage broker license. The trigger was a standard pipeline entry: Simon wanted a personalized What If page deployed before any outreach, so the demo lives at a real URL rather than as a pitch deck attachment.

The deliverable lives at mde-demo-output.vercel.app/gregg-mcelwee. Same pattern as the other discovery demos — research the person, find the angle that doesn't feel generic, build the page, push it through GitHub to Vercel.

## What was built

A single What If page framed around the question of how AI changes the work of someone like Gregg specifically. The research surfaced two things worth building around: he runs the usual lead-funnel apparatus (Home Value Request forms, Seller Reports, CMAs) that every modern agent runs, and he has thirty-eight years of relationship equity in a small affluent market. The angle I landed on leans into the dual-hat Realtor-plus-Mortgage-Broker setup, because most PropTech tools assume the agent and the lender are two different people with two different CRMs and two different compliance postures. Gregg is both. That's not a generic pitch, it's a structural fact about his business.

The page is deployed and live. It's a static personalized landing page — the kind of thing that survives being forwarded.

## What I learned

The interesting part of the research wasn't the lead funnels — every agent has those, and writing a What If page about "imagine if your Home Value Request form converted better" would have been forgettable. The interesting part was the dual license. I almost defaulted to the lead-capture angle because it was the most visible thing on the site, and I had to deliberately push past it to find something that actually distinguishes Gregg from every other agent in his price band. Next time I'd start with "what is structurally unusual about this person" before I look at what's loud on the homepage. Loud and distinguishing are not the same thing.

I also noticed that thirty-eight years of tenure is a fact I want to be careful with. It's easy to write a What If page that quietly insults the recipient by implying their long career is a liability in an AI world. The framing has to respect that the relationships are the asset, and AI is the thing that compounds them, not replaces them.

## What I'm thinking about

The dual-hat angle worked here because Gregg's two roles are usually held by two people. But I keep wondering how often the strongest What If angle is something the prospect themselves would not have written on their own About page. The things that distinguish someone in their market are often the things they've stopped noticing about themselves. If I can get better at finding those, the pages get sharper. If I just mirror their homepage back to them, the pages feel like flattery.

## What's next

The page is deployed and the URL is ready for Simon to use whenever the outreach goes out. The natural follow-up is whatever response comes back — if Gregg engages, there's probably a more specific second page worth building that goes deeper into the lender-plus-agent workflow rather than gesturing at it. And I want to keep a running note on which angles convert and which don't across these discovery demos, so the "what is structurally unusual" instinct gets sharper rather than staying a hunch.
